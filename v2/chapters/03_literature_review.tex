\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:literature-review}

This dissertation advances a single claim across three reliability settings:
detection of a distributional or factual shift is possible exactly when the
learned representation preserves the structure that distinguishes the shift. Two
lenses make that claim precise. The first is \emph{information theory}: what a
representation retains about a target can be quantified as mutual information, and a
shift is detectable only when that quantity is non-zero. The second is
\emph{representation geometry}: the same retention shows up as the arrangement of
features in space, in which directions carry variance and which are collapsed. The
two measure one object, the structure a representation keeps. This chapter is
organized to follow that thesis rather than to staple together three topic surveys.
\Cref{sec:lit:infotheory,sec:lit:representation} establish the shared frame, the
information-theoretic and geometric vocabularies and the bridge between them.
\Cref{sec:lit:ood,sec:lit:hallucination} are then two applications of the same
question: out-of-distribution detection in vision and hallucination detection in
language. \Cref{sec:lit:architectures} reviews the substrate, the architectures and
foundation models whose internal representations the preceding two sections probe.
Throughout, the boundary with the rest of the
document is fixed: \Cref{ch:background} owns the formal \emph{definitions} of the
primitives named here, and each results chapter's related-work section positions
its direct comparators against the placement established in this chapter.

\section{Information Theory in Machine Learning}
\label{sec:lit:infotheory}

Information theory supplies the formal language for ``what a representation
preserves.'' The foundational quantities, entropy, conditional entropy, and
mutual information, originate with \citet{shannon1948mathematical} and are
developed in the standard reference of \citet{cover1999elements}; Fano's
inequality~\citep{robert1952fano} ties them directly to the achievable error of a
predictor, and the infomax principle~\citep{linsker1988self} first proposed
maximizing transmitted information as an objective for learned representations.
This section reviews how these tools have been \emph{used} to understand learning;
\Cref{ch:background} owns their definitions and is assumed here.

\paragraph{The information bottleneck and minimal sufficiency.}
The information bottleneck~\citep{tishby2000information} frames representation
learning as a trade-off between compressing the input and preserving information
about the label, formalising the notion of a \emph{minimal sufficient statistic}
for a prediction task. Applied to deep networks~\citep{tishby2015deep}, it gave
rise to the information-plane account of training~\citep{shwartz2017opening}, in
which layers first fit and then compress; the universality of that compression
phase was subsequently contested~\citep{saxe2019information}, and the debate
clarified that compression is architecture- and nonlinearity-dependent rather than
inevitable. More recent work connects this compression to the geometry of the
learned features~\citep{shwartz2023compress}, an explicit hinge between the two
lenses of this review. The variational information bottleneck~\citep{alemi2017deep}
made the principle trainable at scale, while \citet{achille2018emergence} showed
that minimality and invariance to nuisances are two faces of the same
information-theoretic quantity, and \citet{federici2020learning} extended the
bottleneck to the multi-view setting that underlies contrastive learning.

\paragraph{Estimating mutual information.}
Because mutual information is intractable for high-dimensional learned features, a
literature on variational bounds underpins its use as a training
signal~\citep{poole2019variational}. MINE~\citep{belghazi2018mutual} estimates it
with a neural critic; Deep InfoMax~\citep{hjelm2019learning} maximizes a bound
between local and global features; and the InfoNCE
objective~\citep{oord2018representation} provides the lower bound that the
contrastive methods of \Cref{sec:lit:representation} optimize. This dissertation
relies on that connection directly, since a trainable InfoNCE loss that descends
below its floor demonstrates that the target mutual information is positive.
Complementary to estimation, an information-theoretic account of
generalization~\citep{xu2017information}, building on classical PAC
bounds~\citep{mcallester1999pac}, bounds generalization error by the information a
learned hypothesis retains about its training sample.

Information theory gives the formal language for what a representation preserves
about the label or the shift. The next section gives the geometric one.

\section{Representation Learning and Geometry}
\label{sec:lit:representation}

If \Cref{sec:lit:infotheory} asks \emph{how much} a representation retains, this
section asks \emph{how that retention is arranged}: both how representations are
learned and the geometric structure of what they encode. \Cref{ch:background}
defines the geometric primitives (the feature spectrum, effective rank,
class-discriminative versus shift subspaces, and neural collapse); here we survey
how the field has used them.

\paragraph{Self-supervised and contrastive representation learning.}
Modern representations are dominated by self-supervised objectives that learn
without labels. Pretext-task learning~\citep{gidaris2018unsupervised} gave way to
instance-discriminative contrastive methods, SimCLR~\citep{chen2020simclr,chen2020simple}
and MoCo~\citep{he2020momentum}, and to non-contrastive and masked alternatives
such as DINO~\citep{Caron2021dino}, masked autoencoding~\citep{he2022masked}, and
SimSiam~\citep{chen2021exploring}. The supervised contrastive
loss~\citep{khosla2020supervised} carries the same view-alignment machinery into
the labeled setting, and the paradigm transfers to language through sentence
embeddings~\citep{gao2021simcse}; large-scale self-supervised vision
features~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} now serve as general-purpose backbones. This
lineage descends from a long history of unsupervised representation
learning, principal and independent component analysis and factor
models~\citep{pearson1901liii,hyvarinen2000independent,tipping1999probabilistic,bartholomew1987latent}
and autoencoders~\citep{hinton2006reducing,vincent2008extracting,baldi2012autoencoders}. We
keep it here only as context, since generative latent-variable modeling is
orthogonal to this dissertation's thesis.

\paragraph{The geometry of learned features.}
A second body of work characterizes the \emph{shape} of learned representations.
Neural collapse~\citep{Papyan2020nc} identifies the equiangular-tight-frame
geometry to which classifier features converge in the terminal phase of training,
shown to be the global optimum of the cross-entropy
objective~\citep{Zhu2021nc} and to persist under
transfer~\citep{Galanti2022collapse}; the related dimensional or rank
collapse~\citep{Jing2022rank} describes the loss of feature directions more
broadly. Crucially for this dissertation, \citet{Haas2022ncood} observe that
collapse-adjacent geometry harms out-of-distribution separability, the geometric
counterpart of the information-theoretic claim that compressing away nuisance
directions discards exactly the structure a shift would inhabit. The
alignment-and-uniformity analysis of contrastive
learning~\citep{wang2021understanding} reads a contrastive loss directly as two
geometric properties of the feature sphere, which makes it the clearest bridge
between the two lenses: a quantity defined by mutual information is realised as a
geometric arrangement. Together with the sufficiency--invariance
duality~\citep{achille2018emergence} and the
compression--collapse link~\citep{shwartz2023compress}, these works justify
treating information theory and geometry as one analytic frame rather than two.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation: a cross-cutting thread.}
One line of work matters for two later chapters, so we introduce it here rather
than in either chapter's related work. Knowledge
distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers structure from a teacher
network to a student. Its representation-level variants, contrastive
representation distillation~\citep{tian2020crd}, its language-model
form~\citep{sun2020codir}, and contrastive distillation across augmented
views~\citep{zhang2022cds}, cast distillation as a mutual-information objective
between teacher and student features. \Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse} builds
its method on this lineage (distilling a frozen multi-domain teacher to repopulate
collapsed directions), while \Cref{ch:hallucination-detection} positions its
layer-pair construction \emph{against} it; both back-reference the introduction
given here.

Geometry is the second lens on what a representation keeps. Read through alignment,
uniformity, and collapse, it measures the same structure that information theory
quantifies.

\section{Out-of-Distribution Detection}
\label{sec:lit:ood}

Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection asks whether a test input came from the
training distribution, and it is the setting for two of this dissertation's
contributions: the impossibility result of \Cref{ch:label-blindness} and the
single-domain diagnosis of \Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse}. Read through the
representation lens, every method here is implicitly a bet about which structure the
representation kept.

\paragraph{Post-hoc scorers.}
The dominant paradigm attaches a scoring function to a trained classifier. The
logit- and softmax-family scorers begin with maximum softmax
probability~\citep{hendrycks2016baseline} and extend through input
preprocessing~\citep{liang2017enhancing}, the energy score~\citep{liu2020energy},
and the generalized-entropy score~\citep{liu2023gen}; activation
shaping~\citep{sun2021react,xuscaling} and logit
sparsification~\citep{Sun2022dice} refine them, and large-label-space variants
extend the family to many classes~\citep{huang2021mos}. The distance- or
feature-family scorers instead measure proximity to the training manifold: the
class-conditional Mahalanobis detector~\citep{lee2018simple}, self-supervised
outlier scoring~\citep{sehwag2021ssd}, the residual-space ViM
score~\citep{Wang2022vim}, the decision-boundary distance of
fDBD~\citep{liu2024fdbd}, and nearest-neighbour guidance~\citep{park2023nnguide}.
These scorers excel on multi-domain benchmarks but, as
\Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse} shows, the distance family degrades sharply
when the training data is single-domain, a failure of the representation, not of
the scorer.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and problem framing.}
The task has been consolidated under the generalized-OOD
taxonomy~\citep{yang2021generalized} and the OpenOOD
benchmarks~\citep{yang2022openood,zhang2023openood}, which standardise the near-
versus far-OOD distinction; the broader open-set framing predates deep
learning~\citep{drummond2006open}. \Cref{ch:label-blindness} adds the
\emph{adjacent}-OOD regime to this vocabulary.

\paragraph{Unlabeled, self-supervised, and zero-shot detection.}
A growing literature detects OOD inputs without OOD labels, the setting
\Cref{ch:label-blindness} analyzes. Self-supervised auxiliary tasks improve
robustness and OOD sensitivity~\citep{hendrycks2019using,mohseni2020self},
contrasting shifted instances sharpens the score~\citep{tack2020csi}, and
likelihood-based detectors operate fully unsupervised~\citep{xiao2020likelihood};
recent work pushes unsupervised and training-data-free
detection further~\citep{liu2023unsupervised,du2024and,du2024does}.
Vision--language models enable zero-shot detection from text
alone~\citep{esmaeilpour2022zero,wang2023clipn}. The contribution of
\Cref{ch:label-blindness} is to show that this entire unsupervised family inherits
a structural limit: when the surrogate task is independent of the label-relevant
features, no amount of representation quality can recover the discarded signal.

\paragraph{Single-domain detection, collapse, and transfer.}
The setting closest to \Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse} concerns features that
have lost rank. Neural collapse harms OOD separability~\citep{Haas2022ncood}, and
neural-collapse geometry has itself been turned into a detector in
NECO~\citep{BenAmmar2024neco}, the published method nearest this dissertation's
geometric account and the one \Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse} positions
against most directly. Naive fine-tuning of pretrained features distorts the
geometry that supplies strong OOD baselines~\citep{Kumar2022lp}, contrastive
training can partially preserve it~\citep{Winkens2020contrastive}, and the problem
is acute in specialized single domains such as medical
imaging~\citep{Berger2021medood,zhou2022rethinking}. Orthogonal to the scorer
question, ensemble and Bayesian
methods~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple,daxberger2019bayesian,pmlr-v235-xu24ae}
and the theory of learning under domain
shift~\citep{ben2010theory,vyas2018out,katz2022training} bound what any detector
can achieve when train and test distributions differ.

OOD detection succeeds or fails according to whether the representation retained the
directions that distinguish the shift. This is the recurring subject of
\Cref{ch:label-blindness,ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse}.

\section{Hallucination Detection}
\label{sec:lit:hallucination}

Hallucination detection asks whether a language model's generation is unsupported
by fact, and it is the language-domain instance of the same question: does the
model's internal representation retain the signal that separates a faithful answer
from a fabricated one? This section positions \Cref{ch:hallucination-detection}.

\paragraph{Definitions, taxonomy, and evaluation.}
Surveys organise the phenomenon and its sources~\citep{ji2022survey}, and recent
benchmarks separate detection from generation quality~\citep{bang2025hallulens}.
Evaluation rests on factuality and faithfulness measures: atomic-fact
scoring~\citep{min2023factscore}, curated hallucination
benchmarks~\citep{li2023halueval}, truthfulness
probes~\citep{lin2021truthfulqa,lin2022truthfulqa}, fact
verification~\citep{thorne2018fever}, and tool- or retrieval-augmented
checkers~\citep{peng2023check,chern2023factool}. These supply the operational
labels detectors are scored against.

\paragraph{Sampling- and consistency-based detection.}
One family detects hallucination from output behavior by resampling the model.
SelfCheckGPT~\citep{manakul2023selfcheckgpt} scores the consistency of repeated
generations; semantic entropy~\citep{farquhar2024detecting} clusters samples by
meaning before measuring uncertainty, and its probe form~\citep{kossen2024semantic}
recovers much of the signal from a single pass; energy-based refinements push the
idea beyond entropy~\citep{ma2025semantic}. These methods are accurate but pay a
multiplicative inference cost, the comparison \Cref{ch:hallucination-detection}
makes explicit.

\paragraph{Internal-state probing.}
The family \Cref{ch:hallucination-detection} belongs to reads the model's own
activations, applying the probing paradigm introduced in
\Cref{sec:lit:architectures}. Linear and shallow probes recover truthfulness from
hidden states~\citep{azaria2023internal}, truth-correlated directions can be
identified and even steered~\citep{li2023iti}, and true and false statements
separate geometrically in activation space~\citep{marks2024geometry}; models are
moreover partially calibrated about their own
correctness~\citep{kadavath2022language}, and latent knowledge can be elicited
without labels~\citep{burns2023discovering}. Recent probes exploit richer internal
structure: layer-wise divergence~\citep{zhang2025icr}, cross-layer
attention~\citep{suresh2025clap}, the full activation tensor read as an
image~\citep{barshalom2025actvit}, eigen-analysis of the internal-state
covariance~\citep{chen2024inside}, unlabeled in-the-wild
generations~\citep{du2024haloscope}, attention maps
alone~\citep{chuang2024lookback}, and unsupervised real-time signals from internal
states~\citep{su2024mind}. EigenScore~\citep{chen2024inside} and the
activation-tensor classifier~\citep{barshalom2025actvit} are the nearest published
neighbours to the detector of \Cref{ch:hallucination-detection}, which differs in
deriving its probe from an information-theoretic feasibility argument over
cross-layer activation pairs rather than engineering a scorer.

Hallucination is detectable when the label-relevant signal survives in the model's
intermediate representations. Loss of that structure across layers is the failure
\Cref{ch:hallucination-detection} measures.

\section{Architectures and Foundation Models}
\label{sec:lit:architectures}

The three contributions run on a shared substrate of deep architectures and
pretrained models, and on the interpretability tools used to read their internals.

\paragraph{Backbones.}
Convolutional networks established deep visual
representations~\citep{lecun1998gradient,krizhevsky2012imagenet,he2016deep}, and the
transformer~\citep{vaswani2017attention} and its vision
adaptation~\citep{dosovitskiy2020image,Touvron2021deit} now dominate both
modalities. Foundation models pretrained at scale provide the representations the
detection methods of this dissertation probe: contrastive vision--language
models~\citep{radford2021learning}, large self-supervised vision
backbones~\citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and the language-model
lineage~\citep{radford2018improving,devlin2018bert,brown2020language,raffel2020exploring}.

\paragraph{Probing internal representations.}
Reading what a network encodes in its intermediate layers is a methodology in its
own right, and it is the home of the probing paradigm that
\Cref{sec:lit:representation,sec:lit:hallucination} apply. Linear classifiers
trained on frozen layers diagnose what each layer
encodes~\citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2019analysis}; probing has uncovered
linguistic structure in language models~\citep{tenney2019bert,hewitt2019structural}
and the roles of individual attention heads~\citep{voita2019analyzing}, with an
information-theoretic formulation guarding against probes that merely
memorise~\citep{voita2019information}. Concept- and circuit-level
interpretability~\citep{kim2018interpretability,olah2020zoom} and gradient-based
saliency~\citep{selvaraju2017grad} complete the toolkit the detection chapters use
to establish \emph{where} in a network the shift-distinguishing signal resides.

These architectures are the substrate whose internal representations the OOD and
hallucination chapters interrogate. The question throughout is what those
representations preserve.
