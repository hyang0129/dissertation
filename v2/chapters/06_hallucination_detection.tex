\chapter{Information-Theoretic Hallucination Detection}
\label{ch:hallucination-detection}

% ===================== 6.1 Introduction =====================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:hd:intro}

A large language model that fabricates a confident but false answer has failed in
a way that is invisible at its output: the surface form of a hallucination is
indistinguishable from that of a correct generation. Detecting hallucination from
a model's own internal state has therefore become a central reliability problem,
and a now-substantial literature trains probes on a model's intermediate
activations to flag likely fabrications. This chapter is the third and final
reliability setting of the dissertation, and the one in which the
representation-structure spine appears in its most direct form: where
\Cref{ch:label-blindness} proved \emph{when} a representation cannot support
detection and \Cref{ch:domain-sensitivity-collapse} \emph{restored} a collapsed
representation, here we \emph{detect} the shift directly from the representation
the model leaves behind, and we do so under the information-theoretic lens.

The dominant practice in this literature is to engineer a probe, to search over
architectures, layers, and pooling schemes for whatever reads the activation
tensor best. We take the opposite route and ask whether a detector can be
\emph{derived} from a first principle. The principle is information-theoretic.
Because a transformer's residual stream is deterministic and additive, the mutual
information between the activations at any two layers is positive by construction;
a contrastive objective that treats two layers of a single forward pass as two
views of the same generation is therefore well-posed, in the precise sense that
the signal it tries to extract provably exists. The supervised, one-class form of
that objective targets the slice of the cross-layer information that distinguishes
truthful from hallucinated generations. What makes the argument non-vacuous is not
a further theorem but the simplest possible empirical fact: the loss is trainable.
Because an InfoNCE objective cannot descend when the underlying mutual information
is zero, the descent of our loss \emph{is} the empirical content of the
feasibility claim, the existence of the learned probe is the proof that the
signal is there to be read.

The detector this yields, which we call \emph{Contrastive+Recon}, is a one-class
supervised contrastive probe over layer-pair views of a frozen model, trained
together with an auxiliary logprob-reconstruction objective and read at inference
by a simple distance-to-truthful-bank scorer (\Cref{sec:hd:method}). Evaluated
across two open-weights models and five short-answer benchmarks, it
matches-or-outperforms, in the mean, the strongest engineered probe in the
literature, ACT-ViT, winning some cells cleanly, losing on Natural Questions, and
tying the rest within the seed-to-seed noise band (\Cref{sec:hd:results}). We do
not present this parity as a leaderboard result, and the chapter does not claim to
beat the state of the art. Parity is instead the evidence we want: a detector
built from a first principle, with no architecture search, lands in the same
performance band as one engineered against the benchmark, which is what one would
expect if the principle is the right account of where the signal lives and not
what one would expect if it were wrong. The framework also makes a falsifiable
prediction, that supervising both classes symmetrically should fail when the
hallucinated class has no coherent structure, and the prediction is confirmed by
a sizeable, reproducible drop in detection performance (\Cref{sec:hd:ablations}).
A method that validates its own feasibility premise fits the dissertation's
spine more squarely than one tuned to top the benchmark.

The contributions of this chapter are therefore:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{feasibility argument} for activation-based hallucination detection
  grounded in mutual information: cross-layer mutual information is positive by
  construction, an InfoNCE objective on layer-pair views lower-bounds it, and the
  trainability of that objective is itself the empirical proof that the
  label-relevant signal survives the representation
  (\Cref{subsec:hd:argument}; proofs in \Cref{app:mi-hallucination-proofs}).
  \item \emph{Contrastive+Recon}, a one-class supervised contrastive detector over
  layer-pair views of a frozen language model with an auxiliary logprob-
  reconstruction channel, whose asymmetric construction we show bounds a
  label-conditioned mutual information on the truthful class
  (\Cref{prop:hd:supcon-mi}).
  \item An empirical evaluation establishing \emph{parity} with the strongest
  engineered probe across two models and five datasets, reported honestly
  including the Natural Questions shortfall (\Cref{sec:hd:results}), together with
  a \emph{confirmed theoretical prediction}, the failure of symmetric supervision
  (\Cref{sec:hd:ablations}), and evidence that the learned axis transfers across
  datasets (\Cref{sec:hd:transfer}).
\end{itemize}

The chapter proceeds from the method and its information-theoretic argument
(\Cref{sec:hd:method}), through the experimental setup (\Cref{sec:hd:setup}), the
headline results and the compute-matched comparison
(\Cref{sec:hd:results,sec:hd:compute}), the ablations and signal attribution
(\Cref{sec:hd:ablations}), and cross-dataset transfer (\Cref{sec:hd:transfer}), to
its limitations (\Cref{sec:hd:limitations}).

% ===================== 6.2 Related Work =====================
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:hd:related}

The broad landscape of hallucination, its definitions, its taxonomies, and the
full evaluation apparatus, is surveyed in \Cref{ch:literature-review}, and the
information-theoretic and contrastive primitives this chapter rests on (mutual
information, the InfoNCE bound, and supervised contrast) are introduced in
\Cref{ch:background}. Rather than re-survey that ground, this section positions
the detector of \Cref{sec:hd:method} against the direct comparators it is
measured against in \Cref{sec:hd:results}, and against the contrastive-distillation
precedent it extends. We group the comparators by what they read: an output-space
scalar, a single forward pass of activations, or several sampled generations.

\paragraph{Output-space scalar detectors.}
The cheapest signals discard the model's internal state entirely and score a
generation from its output distribution alone. \citet{kadavath2022language}
establish that language models are partially calibrated, token log-probability,
predictive entropy over the next-token distribution, and an explicit
self-evaluation $P(\mathrm{true})$ each carry usable signal about whether a
generation is correct. We treat these as a legitimate floor rather than a
strawman: they set the lower-compute end of the compute-matched axis of
\Cref{sec:hd:compute}, and any detector that reads the residual stream must
justify the extra access against them.

\paragraph{Activation-space probes.}
The methods architecturally closest to ours read the residual stream from a
single forward pass and learn a head over it. The single-layer linear probe
descends from \citet{alain2017understanding}, who froze a network and trained a
linear classifier on each layer; \citet{belinkov2019analysis} frame this lineage
as a diagnostic methodology that asks \emph{what} a layer encodes rather than how
best to extract a behavior. SAPLMA~\citep{azaria2023internal} replaces the linear
read-out with a fixed multilayer-perceptron head on a single layer's hidden state,
and LLMsKnow~\citep{orgad2024llmsknow} reads internal states to anticipate the
model's own errors. The strongest learned competitor in this family is
ACT-ViT~\citep{barshalom2025actvit}, which treats the full layer-by-token-by-hidden
activation tensor as an image and runs a supervised vision-transformer classifier
over it. ACT-ViT consumes the same multi-layer residual-stream signal we do, so
the comparison in \Cref{sec:hd:results} is one of \emph{how} the multi-layer
structure is exploited, a supervised classifier over a pooled tensor against the
contrastive layer-pair construction of \Cref{sec:hd:method}, not of access. Two
contemporaneous internal-state probes, ICR Probe~\citep{zhang2025icr}, which
summarises each layer with a divergence scalar and runs a multilayer perceptron
over the concatenation, and CLAP~\citep{suresh2025clap}, which applies cross-layer
attention under a binary cross-entropy objective, occupy the same access regime;
neither uses a contrastive layer-pair objective, and we cite them as adjacent work
rather than reproduce them. That activations should carry this signal at all is
grounded causally: \citet{li2023iti} show that shifting activations along
truth-correlated directions changes output truthfulness, \citet{marks2024geometry}
that true and false statements separate near-linearly in late layers, and
\citet{belinkov2019analysis} more broadly that intermediate layers carry
recoverable structure, evidence that motivates reading from the mid-to-late band
of \Cref{sec:hd:setup}.

\paragraph{Sampling-based detectors.}
A third family trades compute for signal by resampling the model. Semantic
entropy~\citep{farquhar2024detecting} draws several generations, clusters them by
bidirectional entailment, and scores the entropy over clusters; SelfCheckGPT
\citep{manakul2023selfcheckgpt} scores the consistency of the original answer
against the additional samples. Its probe form, SEP~\citep{kossen2024semantic},
recovers much of this signal at single-pass cost by regressing the
semantic-entropy score from one forward pass's activations, and is therefore the
nearest direct competitor on the compute axis. Because these methods read several
forward passes, their comparison with the detector studied here is one of compute,
the matched evaluation deferred to \Cref{sec:hd:compute}.

\paragraph{Contrastive distillation as the extended precedent.}
The detector of \Cref{sec:hd:method} borrows the InfoNCE objective
\citep{oord2018representation}, its supervised specialization
\citep{khosla2020supervised}, and the augmentation-view recipe of SimCLR
\citep{chen2020simclr}, all developed in \Cref{ch:background}. The precedent it
most directly extends is contrastive distillation: CRD~\citep{tian2020crd} and its
language-model form CoDIR~\citep{sun2020codir} contrast the \emph{same} layer
across a teacher and a student network, while CDS~\citep{zhang2022cds} applies
InfoNCE to augmented views \emph{within} a single layer. Our construction is
distinct from both. The positive views are different \emph{layers} of one
un-augmented forward pass through a single frozen language model, neither a
cross-network pairing at matched depth nor a within-layer augmentation. To our
knowledge, no prior work uses layer-pair views of a frozen language model as a
one-class contrastive signal; this is the load-bearing novelty, and it is the view
\emph{definition}, not the use of more than one layer, that distinguishes the
method from the activation-space probes above.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:hd:method}

This chapter's claim is that a hallucination detector can be \emph{derived} from
an information-theoretic first principle rather than engineered, and that the
detector's reaching the same performance band as the strongest learned probe is
itself evidence the principle is sound. The argument is deliberately modest in
what it guarantees. It does not promise that a contrastive probe beats every
alternative; it establishes that a contrastive objective on cross-layer views of
a frozen language model is \emph{well-posed}, that the signal it tries to extract
provably exists, and then lets the trainability of the resulting loss stand as
the empirical proof that the construction is non-vacuous. We first fix the
detection task and notation (\Cref{subsec:hd:setup}), then give the feasibility
argument grounded in mutual information (\Cref{subsec:hd:argument}), and finally
specify the concrete detector the argument motivates (\Cref{subsec:hd:detector}).

\subsection{Problem Setup and Notation}
\label{subsec:hd:setup}

We study single-pass white-box hallucination detection. Given a frozen language
model $M$, a prompt $x$, and $M$'s greedy generation $y$, the task is to produce
a scalar score $s(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}$ such that higher scores indicate
hallucinated generations. The label $y_{\mathrm{label}} \in \{0,1\}$ is
operational: $y_{\mathrm{label}} = 1$ when the benchmark's gold-reference
evaluator marks the generation incorrect, and $y_{\mathrm{label}} = 0$
otherwise. We adopt the operational labelling of~\citet{barshalom2025actvit} and
decline to commit to the intrinsic-versus-extrinsic taxonomy
of~\citet{ji2022survey} and~\citet{bang2025hallulens}; the taxonomic placement of
this regime is orthogonal to the architectural argument below, and we return to
it in \Cref{sec:hd:limitations}.

The access regime is read-only: the detector sees $M$'s intermediate
residual-stream activations from the \emph{same} forward pass that generated
$y$. There is no retrieval, no external verifier, and no resampling, those
define the comparison classes of \Cref{sec:hd:setup}. Write
$h_\ell(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^{T_y \times d}$ for the full response-token
hidden-state sequence at layer $\ell$ of $M$'s residual stream while reading
$(x,y)$, with $L$ total layers, $T_y$ response tokens, and hidden dimension
$d = 4096$ for both supported models. We extract from a mid-to-late residual-
stream band $[\ell_{\mathrm{lo}}, \ell_{\mathrm{hi}}]$, motivated below by the
evidence that truth-relevant structure concentrates there~\citep{li2023iti,marks2024geometry}.
Two facts about $h_\ell$ are load-bearing. First, $h_\ell$ is \emph{deterministic}
given $(x,y)$: the forward pass is a fixed function of its inputs. Second,
$h_\ell$ is a \emph{sequence over response tokens}, not a single vector, the
method consumes the entire per-token activation trajectory $t = 1,\ldots,T_y$ at
each layer, not the last-token activation alone. The label $y_{\mathrm{label}}$,
by contrast, may carry evaluator noise, a point we carry forward to
\Cref{sec:hd:limitations}.

Because the method reads activations from a single forward pass, its comparison
with multi-sample methods such as semantic entropy and SelfCheckGPT is one of
\emph{compute}, not of \emph{signal access}: those methods resample $M$ several
times, whereas the detectors studied here, and the strongest learned baselines
they are measured against, all read one forward pass. We make this explicit here
so the compute-matched comparison of \Cref{sec:hd:compute} reads naturally rather
than cold.

\subsection{Why a Learned Cross-Layer Compression Can Carry the Signal}
\label{subsec:hd:argument}

The cleanest argument for why this could work is not a geometric story about
where truth lives in activation space; it is a feasibility argument grounded in
mutual information. We can write down a well-posed contrastive objective on
layer-pair views because the cross-layer mutual information is positive by
construction, and the empirical fact that the loss is trainable on our data is
itself the proof that the argument is not vacuous. The argument has three coupled
claims and one punchline.

\paragraph{Claim 1: cross-layer mutual information is positive by construction.}
For any two layers $\ell_a < \ell_b$ in $M$'s residual stream and a fixed
response, the activation sequence $h_{\ell_b}$ is a deterministic function of
$h_{\ell_a}$ together with the intervening residual blocks, with the residual
connections explicitly carrying earlier-layer information forward. The
data-processing chain of \Cref{lem:hd:dpi} then gives
$I(h_{\ell_a}; h_{\ell_b}) > 0$ for any input distribution that varies
non-trivially across $h_{\ell_a}$. The mutual information here is between
\emph{sequence-valued} objects, full per-token activation tensors at two
layers, which makes the architectural argument stronger, not weaker: there are
more shared channels, not fewer. A sharper form is available. Under the same
downward-determinism assumption, \Cref{cor:hd:coinfo} collapses the three-way
interaction information to
$I(h_{\ell_a}; h_{\ell_b}; y) = I(h_{\ell_b}; y) > 0$, so the cross-layer shared
information also overlaps the response variable $y$: the joint is non-orthogonal
to what the model generated. One caveat is essential and we state it plainly.
This is an architectural statement about the response $y$, not the label
$y_{\mathrm{label}}$; since a single layer's $h_\ell$ is a lossy compression of
$(x,y)$, \Cref{cor:hd:coinfo} \emph{upper}-bounds the label-relevant interaction
information but does not lower-bound it. Whether the label-relevant features
survive that compression is an empirical question, owned by Claim~2 and the
trainability punchline.

\paragraph{Claim 2: supervised InfoNCE bounds a label-conditioned mutual information.}
The loss family we use is InfoNCE, whose standard variational form
(\Cref{lem:hd:infonce}, following~\citet{oord2018representation} and the
tightness analysis of~\citet{poole2019variational}) lower-bounds the mutual
information between two views. We run its supervised
specialization~\citep{khosla2020supervised} with one critical asymmetry: the
hallucinated class is excluded from mutual-positive grouping (the
\texttt{ignore\_label} scheme detailed in \Cref{subsec:hd:detector}). Under this
asymmetry the optimized bound becomes \emph{one-sided}, a bound on the mutual
information between the learned features and the truthful class
($y_{\mathrm{label}} = 0$), because the loss pulls truthful-class features into a
coherent cluster while hallucinated-class features are required only to be
view-consistent with themselves. We make this precise in
\Cref{prop:hd:supcon-mi}; the alignment-uniformity decomposition
of~\citet{wang2021understanding} gives a complementary geometric reading of what
the loss optimizes.

\begin{proposition}[Asymmetric supervised contrast bounds truthful-class mutual information]\label{prop:hd:supcon-mi}
Let $Z = f_\theta(h)$ be the learned feature and consider the supervised
contrastive objective under the asymmetric \texttt{ignore\_label} scheme of
\Cref{subsec:hd:detector}: truthful anchors take other truthful samples and their
own other-layer views as positives, while hallucinated anchors take only their
own other-layer views as positives and treat all other samples, including other
hallucinated samples, as negatives. Then the optimized objective lower-bounds
$I\bigl(Z; \mathbb{1}[y_{\mathrm{label}} = 0]\bigr)$ together with an
instance-level view-consistency term, and contributes \emph{no} bound on
$I\bigl(Z; \mathbb{1}[y_{\mathrm{label}} = 1]\bigr)$. The bound is one-sided: it
constrains the truthful class only. The proof is given in
\Cref{app:mi-hallucination-proofs}.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Claim 3: the supervised objective selects the label-relevant slice.}
Claim~1 establishes that the joint $(h_{\ell_a}, h_{\ell_b})$ carries shared
information; most of it, the prompt's surface form, the topic, the
token-position structure, is irrelevant to whether $y$ is hallucinated. The
supervised contrastive objective of Claim~2 selects the label-relevant slice of
that shared information by making the truthful-class views collapse to a coherent
cluster while leaving hallucinated views to repel everything. This is the
structural reason supervised contrast on layer pairs is a sensible extractor: not
a guarantee of empirical superiority, but a guarantee that the construction
targets the right slice of the joint.

\paragraph{The punchline: feasibility is established by trainability.}
Everything in Claims~1--3 would be vacuous if $I(h_{\ell_a}; h_{\ell_b})$ were
zero, or if the label-relevant subspace were empty. The cleanest empirical
confirmation that neither holds is the simplest one: our loss descends. The
contrastive loss bounds $I(z_a; z_b)$ for the compressed embeddings
$z = f_\theta(h)$, and the data-processing inequality (\Cref{lem:hd:dpi}) gives
$I(z_a; z_b) \le I(h_{\ell_a}; h_{\ell_b})$, so any positive mutual information
observed at the embedding level is direct evidence of positive mutual information
at the activation level, the inequality runs in our favor. By
\Cref{lem:hd:infonce}, if $I(\mathrm{view}_a; \mathrm{view}_b) = 0$ then no
InfoNCE variant can descend below $\log K$, the value attained by uniform
negatives. The fact that the training loss drops below that floor and the
embeddings cluster meaningfully is the operational proof that the cross-layer
mutual information we are extracting is positive and that the supervised slice we
isolate is non-empty. The existence of the learned probe is therefore the
empirical content of the feasibility argument, not merely consistent with it.

\paragraph{What the argument does not claim.}
Three honest negatives bound the argument's reach, and one positive prediction
sharpens it. It does not claim the contrastive subspace beats every other
extractor, that is the empirical question \Cref{sec:hd:results} answers. It does
not claim cross-layer views are uniquely good relative to, say, same-layer
dropout views or augmented-input views; the layer-pair sensitivity sweep of
\Cref{sec:hd:ablations} is what speaks to which pairs carry the signal, and the
choice of different layers as views is part principled (the residual-stream
evidence of \Cref{subsec:hd:setup}) and part empirical bet, which we say
plainly. And it does not predict the \emph{magnitude} of any accuracy gain, which
is set by the data, not the bound. What it does predict is a specific failure
mode: \Cref{cor:hd:symcon} states that supervising both classes symmetrically,
when the hallucinated class lacks coherent latent structure, injects gradient
noise negatively correlated with the truthful-class signal. The symmetric-scheme
ablation of \Cref{sec:hd:ablations} confirms this, with an AUROC drop of roughly
ten points. The prediction came before the test, not after it.

\subsection{The Detector: Supervised Contrast over Layer Pairs with Logprob Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:hd:detector}

The feasibility argument turns into four concrete artifacts: the activations we
extract, the network that compresses them, the loss that pulls the
label-relevant signal out, and the scorer that turns a learned embedding into a
hallucination score.

\paragraph{Activation extraction.}
From each $(x,y)$ we extract
$\{ h_\ell(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^{T_y \times d} : \ell \in [\ell_{\mathrm{lo}}, \ell_{\mathrm{hi}}] \}$,
the full per-token response activation sequence at every layer in the
mid-to-late band. No last-token reduction is applied at extraction time; the
entire trajectory is retained per layer and per generation, and cached once per
model, dataset, and split so that training across seeds reuses cached tensors
without re-extraction. Keeping the full response sequence is load-bearing: it
gives the compressor a sequence to pool over and gives the reconstruction target
a per-token logprob signal to regress against.

\paragraph{The compression network.}
A shared progressive compressor $f_\theta$ maps each layer's per-token activation
sequence to a single embedding. The sequence $(T_y, d)$ passes through an input
normalization and dropout, a sinusoidal positional encoding, and a stack of
transformer-encoder blocks that halve the hidden width at each step
($4096 \to 2048 \to 1024 \to 512$); a mean-pool over the response-token
dimension and a linear projection yield $z \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ per generation
and layer. One structural fact matters for the argument: the compressor is
\emph{shared} across layers, one $f_\theta$, not per-layer heads, so the layer
pair is the only source of view asymmetry. The full architectural specification
(block count, dimension schedule, dropout) is deferred to the public
implementation. We note in advance of \Cref{sec:hd:results} that at matched
parameter count the method reaches or exceeds a single-layer multilayer-
perceptron probe, which rules out raw capacity as the explanation for its
performance.

\paragraph{View construction.}
Each training example yields two views by sampling a layer pair
$(\ell_a, \ell_b)$ from the cached band: each view is one layer's full activation
tensor passed through $f_\theta$ to a $512$-dimensional embedding. The two views
of a generation are therefore two compressed vectors, each summarizing the full
per-token activation sequence at one layer of $M$. This is the structural
novelty: the positive views are \emph{different layers of the same un-augmented
forward pass}, not data augmentations and not different networks.

\paragraph{The supervised contrastive loss with asymmetric labelling.}
The core objective is supervised contrastive~\citep{khosla2020supervised} with
one deliberate asymmetry. For a truthful anchor ($y_{\mathrm{label}} = 0$), all
other truthful samples in the batch are positives and all hallucinated samples
are negatives. For a hallucinated anchor ($y_{\mathrm{label}} = 1$), the
positives are \emph{only} the anchor's own other-layer views; everything else in
the batch, including other hallucinated samples, is a negative. The reason for
this is not that hallucinations form no coherent class; the ablations of
\Cref{sec:hd:ablations} show that either class can serve as the one-class anchor
nearly as well. The correct reading is that labelling \emph{both} classes
simultaneously forces a second coherent cluster the data does not support. The
symmetric scheme's gradient includes a term pulling hallucinated embeddings
toward one another, and when the hallucinated class lacks coherent latent
structure that term is noise that actively fights the truthful-class gradient
(\Cref{cor:hd:symcon}). The asymmetry is therefore a structural choice, one-class
representation learning with the labeled class as the inlier anchor, and not an
arbitrary implementation preference. Structurally, the loss is a
representation-learning form of one-class anomaly detection on truthful samples,
with cross-layer view-consistency supplying a second source of supervision for
both classes; this is exactly what makes the truthful-class mutual-information
specialization of \Cref{prop:hd:supcon-mi} the correct theoretical lens.

\paragraph{Logprob-reconstruction auxiliary loss.}
From the compressed feature $z = f_\theta(h_\ell) \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$, a small
decoder $g_\phi$ (a two-layer perceptron, $512 \to 256 \to 64$) predicts a
fixed-length proxy for the per-token logprob sequence of $y$ under $M$:
\begin{equation} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}} = \big\| g_\phi(z) - \mathrm{resample}\big( (\log p_M(y_t \mid y_{<t}, x))_{t=1}^{T_y},\ R \big) \big\|_2^2, \label{eq:hd:recon} \end{equation}
where the variable-length logprob sequence is linearly interpolated to the fixed
length $R = 64$ that the decoder outputs. A variance-suppression guard zeroes
this term on batches whose logprob target has near-constant variance, preventing
the decoder from spending capacity on degenerate sequences. The rationale for the
target is causal: when $M$ is about to fabricate, its next-token distribution
typically broadens and the top-token probability drops, so per-token logprobs are
coupled to the hallucination event. The reconstruction loss thus applies
regression-style supervision that retains features predictive of a
label-correlated signal, complementary to the classification-style supervision
the contrastive loss provides. It is not the same as a semantic-entropy
probe~\citep{kossen2024semantic}, which regresses a single scalar per
generation: here the target is a length-$R$ per-token logprob sequence decoded
from a representation that already aggregates the full activation tensor.

\paragraph{Total objective and inference.}
The total loss combines the two channels,
\begin{equation} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SupCon\text{-}asym}} + \lambda\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}}, \label{eq:hd:total} \end{equation}
with the weight $\lambda$ and the contrastive temperature fixed early and left
untuned, so the channel-attribution study of \Cref{sec:hd:ablations}, not a
weight sweep, is the meaningful sensitivity analysis. At inference the trained
$f_\theta$ embeds a held-out $(x,y)$ and scores it by its distance to the
training-set truthful-class embedding bank; a $k$-nearest-neighbour distance is
the headline scorer, with cosine-to-centroid and Mahalanobis distances reported
as alternatives in \Cref{sec:hd:ablations}. All three are inference-only post-hoc
scorers on the same learned $f_\theta$ and require no retraining. Because two
distinct supervision channels are present, the chapter owes an attribution of the
gain to one, the other, or both; \Cref{sec:hd:ablations} pays this off with the
$2 \times 2$ ablation the method's design anticipates.

% ===================== 6.4 Experimental Setup =====================
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:hd:setup}

This section fixes the evaluation grid: the models whose residual streams the detectors read, the short-answer benchmarks on which hallucinations are scored, the comparison methods grouped by the signal they consume, the per-cell training protocol, and the single metric the main text reports. The detection task, the operational label $y_{\mathrm{label}}$, and the read-only single-forward-pass access regime were fixed in \Cref{sec:hd:method} and are not restated here; this section enumerates what we ran.

\paragraph{Models.}
We evaluate on two $8$B-scale open-weights instruction-tuned models drawn from distinct training pipelines: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct~\citep{grattafiori2024llama3} and Qwen3-8B~\citep{yang2025qwen3}. Both are transformer decoders with hidden dimension $d = 4096$, matching the $d$ assumed throughout \Cref{sec:hd:method}. Qwen3-8B is run in its non-thinking mode (single-pass, no chain-of-thought) for parity with Llama. We use two distinct lineages rather than one so that the parity claim of \Cref{sec:hd:results} cannot be read as an artifact of a single training recipe; we do not scale to larger or closed models, a scoping choice we revisit in \Cref{sec:hd:limitations}.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
The detectors are evaluated on five open-domain, short-answer question-answering benchmarks. HotpotQA~\citep{yang2018hotpotqa} supplies multi-hop factoid questions over Wikipedia passages, used in the closed-book distractor setting. Natural Questions~\citep{kwiatkowski2019natural} contributes open-domain questions mined from real search queries, restricted to short-answer items. PopQA~\citep{mallen2023popqa} is an entity-popularity-stratified factoid set that emphasises long-tail entities, where a retrieval-free model is more prone to fabricate. SciQ~\citep{welbl2017sciq} provides crowd-authored science multiple-choice questions with the correct option as the gold target. SearchQA~\citep{dunn2017searchqa} contributes Jeopardy!-style questions, which we take strictly closed-book with the accompanying search snippets discarded. Consistent with the operational labelling of \Cref{sec:hd:method}, a generation is treated as hallucinated when the gold-reference evaluator marks it incorrect, applied uniformly across all five benchmarks. We do not tabulate per-dataset sizes or hallucination rates here.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against twelve methods spanning three families, each consuming a different signal and grouped here by the compute it draws. The first family is output-space scalar scorers read from the same greedy generation pass: sequence log-probability (LogProb), token entropy, and the prompt-based P(true) self-evaluation~\citep{kadavath2022language}. The second family is activation-space probes that read a single forward pass: a single-layer linear probe~\citep{alain2017understanding}, the SAPLMA multilayer-perceptron head~\citep{azaria2023internal}, the layer-and-token-sweeping LLMsKnow probe~\citep{orgad2024llmsknow}, and ACT-ViT~\citep{barshalom2025actvit}, the strongest learned competitor and the one architecturally closest to the detector of \Cref{subsec:hd:detector}. The third family is sampling-based: length-normalized and semantic-entropy variants~\citep{farquhar2024detecting,kossen2024semantic} together with SelfCheckGPT in its NLI and $n$-gram forms~\citep{manakul2023selfcheckgpt}. The compute axis separates the families cleanly: the scalar and activation-probe methods, like Contrastive+Recon, read one forward pass, whereas the sampling methods resample $M$ several times ($K = 10$). This partition is the structural prerequisite for the compute-matched comparison of \Cref{sec:hd:compute}.

\paragraph{Training.}
Each trained probe is fit per $(\text{model}, \text{dataset})$ cell on that dataset's training split only, over $5$ random seeds, with mean $\pm$ standard deviation reported across them; no probe is trained on more than one dataset, and the held-out test split is fixed across methods and seeds within a cell. Cross-dataset training is never used here, transfer to unseen datasets is evaluated separately in \Cref{sec:hd:transfer} by applying a source-trained checkpoint to a target test split with no retraining. We give no hyperparameters inline and instead forward the reader to the public implementation for the full training recipe.

\paragraph{Metric.}
AUROC, computed against the operational hallucination label and reported as the mean $\pm$ standard deviation over $5$ seeds, is the only metric carried in the main text, for both the headline table of \Cref{sec:hd:results} and the transfer matrix of \Cref{sec:hd:transfer}. Complementary metrics (AUPRC, expected calibration error, and FPR@95) are relegated to the supplementary material and not reported here.

% ===================== 6.7 Ablations / Signal Attribution =====================
\section{Ablations and Signal Attribution}
\label{sec:hd:ablations}

The detector of \Cref{sec:hd:method} joins two supervision channels over a shared
compressor, an asymmetric one-class supervised contrastive loss on layer-pair
views, and a logprob-reconstruction auxiliary, and reads the result with a
distance scorer. This section asks what carries the signal. We lead with the one
question the theory makes falsifiable, because it is the strongest evidence that
the feasibility argument is the right account and not merely a consistent one,
and then frame the channel-attribution structure the method's design anticipates.

\paragraph{The confirmed prediction: symmetric supervision fails.}
The asymmetric construction of \Cref{subsec:hd:detector}, labelling only the
truthful class as a mutual-positive cluster and leaving hallucinated anchors to be
view-consistent with themselves, is not an arbitrary implementation choice. The
analysis of \Cref{cor:hd:symcon} predicts that the symmetric alternative, which
relabels the hallucinated side as a second mutual-positive cluster, must
\emph{degrade} detection whenever the hallucinated class lacks coherent latent
structure, because the hallucinated-anchor gradient then becomes noise negatively
correlated with the truthful-cluster signal. This is a prediction made before the
measurement, and it is confirmed: replacing the asymmetric scheme with symmetric
supervision drops AUROC by roughly ten points on both models, far outside the
seed-to-seed band of \Cref{sec:hd:results}. A wrong account of \emph{why} the loss
is well-posed would not have forecast a specific, large, reproducible failure of a
natural variant; that it did is the chapter's clearest instance of theory
predicting experiment rather than rationalising it. The direction of the one-class
anchor, by contrast, is a secondary choice: anchoring on the hallucinated rather
than the truthful side performs comparably, so the load-bearing decision is that
\emph{one} class is left as the unlabeled outlier, not which one.

\paragraph{Signal attribution across the two channels.}
The two supervision channels invite a $2\times 2$ attribution: present or absent
contrastive structure, crossed with present or absent logprob reconstruction. The
cell with neither is the single-layer supervised multilayer-perceptron probe
(SAPLMA) of \Cref{sec:hd:setup}; the full method is the cell with both; the two
single-channel cells isolate each contribution. We frame this matrix here as the
structure the design anticipates, but we do not resolve its cells numerically in
this chapter: an exhaustive per-cell attribution, and the matched-parameter and
scorer sweeps below, belongs to the experimental record accompanying the method
rather than to the dissertation's argument, and we flag the per-channel
decomposition as the natural next measurement rather than claim it here.

\paragraph{Supervised structure, not capacity or augmentation.}
Two controls bound what the result can be attributed to. A label-free variant of
the same architecture, cross-layer contrast with no asymmetric labels, the
direct analogue of an unsupervised SimCLR recipe applied to layer pairs, lands
near chance, which shows that the hallucination axis is not surfaced by
cross-layer contrast alone: the supervision, not merely the layer-pair view
construction, is what selects the label-relevant slice. And because the compressor
reaches its performance at a parameter count no larger than the single-layer
multilayer-perceptron probe it is measured against (\Cref{subsec:hd:detector}),
raw capacity is not the explanation either. What carries the signal is the
\emph{supervised, one-class} structure the feasibility argument prescribes.

\paragraph{Scorer choice.}
The headline inference scorer is a nearest-neighbour distance to the truthful-class
embedding bank; cosine distance to the truthful centroid and a Mahalanobis
distance under the pooled covariance are alternatives on the same frozen
representation and yield comparable rankings. The scorer is thus an empirical
convenience applied post-hoc to a fixed embedding, not a load-bearing part of the
method.

This section asks the transfer question directly: when a probe is moved to an unseen target dataset with no retraining, does the axis it learned still separate hallucinations, or was it specific to the source's surface form? % ===================== 6.9 Limitations =====================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:hd:limitations}

The chapter's central claim is deliberately bounded, and several limitations
delimit it further. The result is \emph{parity}, not dominance: Contrastive+Recon
sits in the same performance band as the strongest engineered probe rather than
above it, and the contribution is the information-theoretic principle and its
self-validation, not a benchmark win. The most concrete cost of that honesty is
Natural Questions, where the detector trails ACT-ViT on both models, firmly on
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and where the learned axis also transfers least well
(\Cref{sec:hd:results,sec:hd:transfer}); we have no account of the NQ shortfall
beyond noting that it is the one benchmark on which the principle and the
engineered probe come apart, and explaining it is open work.

Several further limits are worth stating plainly. The operational label is
produced by an automatic gold-reference evaluator, so a fraction of what we score
as hallucination is evaluator artefact rather than model error; the AUROC ceiling
is correspondingly noisy, and this noise is heaviest exactly where gold answers
are least clean. We decline to place the detection regime within the
intrinsic-versus-extrinsic hallucination taxonomy, treating that placement as
orthogonal to the architectural argument; a reader who wants the taxonomy resolved
will not find it here. The evaluation spans two open-weights models at the
eight-billion-parameter scale and five short-answer question-answering benchmarks;
we do not test larger or closed models, long-form generation, or multilingual
settings, and the single-pass white-box access the method requires is unavailable
for API-only models. The channel attribution of \Cref{sec:hd:ablations} is left as
a framework rather than resolved numerically, so which of the two supervision
channels carries how much of the signal is not settled here. Finally, the main
text reports AUROC alone; complementary metrics (AUPRC, expected calibration
error, and FPR@95) and the contemporaneous probes we cite but do not reproduce,
such as ICR Probe, are relegated to supplementary material and to related work
respectively.
