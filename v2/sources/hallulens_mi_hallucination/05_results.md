## 5. Main Results

### 5.1 Headline table

**Framing sentence.** AUROC ± std (5 seeds) for every (model, dataset, method) cell across the 2 × 5 × 10 grid of §4. The table is the single most reviewer-visible artifact in the paper; every cell ties back to a `\result` macro and a config in [`configs/experiments/`](../configs/experiments/). The prose immediately following the table reads the table — it does not re-summarize the methods.

**Bullets — write as 1–2 sentences in prose each (except the *cell-by-cell narrative* bullet, which gets a paragraph):**

- **Table layout.** Two horizontally stacked subtables, one per model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B). Rows = methods grouped by §4.3 class (output-space scalar K=1 / activation-space K=1 / sampling K=10), with a horizontal rule between groups. Columns = the five datasets in the order HotpotQA, NQ, PopQA, SciQ, SearchQA. **Bolding rule:** within each column, bold the *single* highest-mean cell *only when its 5-seed mean exceeds the runner-up by more than max(stds) of the two cells*; otherwise call the cells tied and bold neither. This rule is load-bearing — many ours-vs-ACT-ViT margins are inside one-std overlap and the prose must not over-bold. State the bolding rule in the table caption.

- **Reporting unit.** Each cell is `mean ± std` over 5 seeds, sourced via `\resultPM{headline_results}{<model>:<dataset>:<method>:auroc:mean,...:auroc:std}[3]`. All cells are 5/5 seeds as of the 2026-05-21 refreshed `results/draft_headline_table.md`; no `*N/5` footnotes expected.

- **What the table is supposed to *show* — three nested claims, prose orders them outermost-first.**
  1. **Across-cluster dominance.** Activation-space (K=1) methods cleanly beat output-space scalar (K=1) and sampling (K=10) methods on every cell where both are reportable — the headline gap between the activation cluster and the sampling cluster is order-of-magnitude `0.15–0.25 AUROC` `[TEMP — re-verify from headline_results.csv over the five paper datasets]` across both models. This is the cleanest single sentence in the section.
  2. **Within-cluster: ours vs. the strongest learned baselines.** Among activation-space methods, the comparison is tight per-cell but favorable in the mean: ours ≈ best-individual-learned-baseline `+ 0.03–0.05 AUROC` `[TEMP — re-verify from headline_results.csv]` averaged over the 10 cells (2 models × 5 datasets), with the strongest competitor (ACT-ViT) currently ≈ `0.04` `[TEMP]` behind in mean. The prose verb is **"matches-or-outperforms"** for learned baselines per [`outline.md:28`](outline.md#L28); do not escalate to "beats."
  3. **Per-cell honesty.** Of 10 cells, ours wins clearly (margin > both stds) on **PopQA on both models** and **SciQ on Qwen** (with SciQ-Llama close); loses on both NQ cells to ACT-ViT (Llama `−0.013`, Qwen `−0.018`; both 5/5 — these losses are firm) `[TEMP — re-verify deltas from headline_results.csv]`; is tied with ACT-ViT within `±0.02` on HotpotQA, SearchQA, and within `±0.01` of Linear/LLMsKnow on the smaller wins (Llama-PopQA, Qwen-PopQA) `[TEMP — all bands re-verify]`. The §5.1 prose says this in one sentence and forwards the NQ shortfall to §8.

- **Cell-by-cell narrative — one paragraph after the table.** The paragraph follows a fixed template: (1) name the cluster gap (sentence 1, headline number from claim 1 above); (2) name the within-cluster summary (sentence 2, mean-margin number from claim 2); (3) name the clean wins — **PopQA (both models)** and **SciQ-Qwen** — with current draft margins `+0.005 to +0.010` over the best learned baseline on PopQA and `+0.029` over ACT-ViT on SciQ-Qwen `[TEMP — re-verify from headline_results.csv]`; (4) name the two clean losses (NQ-Llama, NQ-Qwen) and forward to §8; (5) name the within-band ties and forward to §7 ablations and §7.4 scorer for any cell whose interpretation depends on scorer choice. This paragraph is ~5 sentences and does no more than read the table — interpretive freight goes to §8.

- **Supplementary cells.** Output-space scalar baselines (LogProb, Token Entropy) are deterministic single-seed cells and are fully populated across the five paper datasets × two models (no seed variation). They appear in the table as single-value rows (no ± std) and feed the cluster-gap headline number in claim 1; the §5 prose does not interpret them per-cell.

**What §5.1 establishes for the rest of the paper.** The *number* that the abstract and §1 contribution-bullets quote, the *cell* set that §6 transfer and §7 ablations sit relative to, and the *honest call-out* of the NQ shortfall that §8 picks up. ### 5.3 Compute-matched comparison

**Framing sentence.** The cleanest single comparison in the paper: at single forward pass (K=1, ours), AUROC matches or beats every K=10 sampling-based method by 0.15–0.25 AUROC across the five datasets, both models. This is the comparison the §1 / abstract compute-efficiency claim sits on, and the comparison §5.3 spends most of its budget making vivid.

**Bullets — write as 1–2 sentences in prose each:**

- **Figure type.** One panel per dataset (5 panels: HotpotQA, NQ, PopQA, SciQ, SearchQA). Within each panel, a scatter plot with **AUROC on the y-axis** and **forward-pass count on the x-axis (log scale, 1 / 2 / 10 ticks marked)**. Each method is a single marker positioned at its (compute, AUROC) point. Both models on each panel, distinguished by marker shape (Llama = circle, Qwen = square). The visual story is: top-left is best; ours sits at K=1 with high AUROC; SE / SelfCheck cluster sits at K=10 with materially lower AUROC.

- **Methods included on the figure.**
  - **K=1 markers:** ours (Contrastive+Recon), single-layer linear probe, LLMsKnow probe, SAPLMA, ACT-ViT, P(true) at K=2.
  - **K=10 markers:** SE-length-norm (headline sampling scorer), SelfCheckGPT-NLI (headline sampling scorer).
  - SE-semantic and SelfCheck-ngram are reported in the supplementary scatter (Appendix C).

- **Headline number for the §5.3 paragraph (subject to numbers freeze).** Across the five datasets and both models, the mean AUROC of ours at K=1 exceeds the mean AUROC of SE-length-norm at K=10 by ≈ `+0.18–0.21 AUROC` (Llama ≈ `+0.21`, Qwen ≈ `+0.18`); the same comparison against SelfCheckGPT-NLI at K=10 gives ≈ `+0.16–0.21 AUROC` (Llama ≈ `+0.21`, Qwen ≈ `+0.16`) `[TEMP — every delta and mean here re-verifies from headline_results.csv]`. The prose drops one of these as the §1 / abstract anchor — recommend Llama × SE-length-norm (`+0.21`) as the cleanest single number, or report the both-models mean (`+0.18` vs. SE, `+0.18` vs. SelfCheck) as a single statistic.

- **What the figure is *not*.** It is not a compute-vs-accuracy Pareto curve in the broader sense (we do not vary K within our method, do not vary capacity within baselines). It is a specific operational comparison: *at the K each method's authors recommend, our K=1 beats every K=10 sampling method*. State this scope in the caption — and forward the "what if K=20 sampling closes the gap" reviewer concern to §9 limitations (already named there in spirit; consider a one-sentence pre-rebuttal in the §5.3 paragraph if the page budget allows).

Naming this number in the prose is the strongest single defense of the compute-efficiency story.

### 5.4 Calibration

**Framing sentence.** Reliability diagrams for one (model, dataset) cell per model. ECE numbers across the full 10-cell grid (2 models × 5 datasets) live in Appendix C; §5.4 in the main paper carries the qualitative visual only.

**Bullets — write as 1 sentence each:**

- **Cells shown.** One reliability diagram per model. Default choice: HotpotQA-Llama and PopQA-Qwen, on the grounds that these are the two cells with the cleanest class balance and the highest absolute AUROC (so the calibration story is interpretable rather than dominated by class-imbalance artifacts). Lock the choice after `paper/data/calibration.csv` lands; recommend re-checking against ECE numbers before fixing.

- **Diagram format.** Standard reliability diagram: x-axis = predicted hallucination probability (binned, 10 bins), y-axis = empirical hallucination rate within bin, dashed diagonal as the perfect-calibration reference, bar widths proportional to bin counts. Overlay ours and one strong learned baseline (recommend ACT-ViT, since it is the §5.1 / §5.2 named comparator throughout §5) on the same diagram, distinguished by hatch pattern.

- **Score → probability mapping.** All scorers (KNN, cosine, Mahalanobis, ViT logits, MLP logits) are scalar outputs that are *not* native probabilities; we map to `[0, 1]` via the empirical CDF on the *training-set* scores per cell (already stated in [`04_experimental_setup.md`](04_experimental_setup.md) §4.5 ECE bullet). State this mapping once in the §5.4 caption and forward to Appendix C for the alternative isotonic / Platt-scaling sensitivity analysis.

- **What the diagram is *not*.** It is not an argument that our method is well-calibrated *out of the box* — it almost certainly is not, and the §5.4 prose says so. The argument is operational: even with a coarse empirical-CDF mapping, the predicted score is monotone-faithful enough to threshold against (FPR@95 reported in Appendix C). The §8 discussion may pick this up if calibration is unexpectedly poor.

- **Pending data.** `paper/data/calibration.csv` does not yet exist; the calibration pipeline needs an aggregator script — see open question 3 below. **§5.4 prose is the second-most-blocked subsection after §5.1** on data availability.

**What §5.4 establishes for the rest of the paper.** Modest claim about score usefulness, not strong claim about calibration. Reader exits §5.4 knowing the predicted score is reasonable-but-not-perfect, ECE numbers live in Appendix C, and the §5 main paper is done.
